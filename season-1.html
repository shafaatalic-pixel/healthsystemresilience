§ 01 · The central question

Can a health system be understood as economic infrastructure?

Season 1 answers yes, and shows what follows for households, institutions, and public trust when that infrastructure holds or fails.

Across two countries and nine pieces, one argument: the resilience of a health system is not a humanitarian afterthought but a form of economic protection. When illness, debt, disaster, or workforce collapse strike, resilient systems protect livelihoods; fragile ones transmit the shock into households and budgets.

§ 03 · Impact & methodology

Engagement as advocacy intelligence.

The campaign drew 57,274 impressions with no paid promotion, 5.7× its 10,000-impression success criterion. Eleven findings came out of that data. All eleven are recorded below.

What was measured: impressions, audience geography and composition, and platform routing, compiled from platform-native analytics (Meta, LinkedIn, Instagram) and reconciled at >94% against full-campaign locks; Instagram adjusted for cross-post referral traffic.

§ 04 · The findings record

Eleven findings from the Season 1 data.

Documented patterns in how these arguments moved, to be tested again in Season 2. The four headline findings follow claim · evidence · interpretation; the rest are logged as observations and mechanisms.
FINDING 01
Tagging deepens a national audience rather than broadening it.
Claim
Tagging named officials amplifies reach.
Evidence
≈3× median impressions on tagged Daily Star pieces, replicated five weeks apart; 76-80% Bangladesh audience.
Interpretation
Tagging is a repeatable amplifier, but it concentrates a home audience. It does not cross borders.
FINDING 02
The same content reaches opposite audiences on different platforms.
Claim
Platform, not content, was the strongest predictor of who saw an argument.
Evidence
Same slider, same day: Facebook routed to Bangladesh; LinkedIn to 61% health-domain U.S. professionals.
Interpretation
A binational platform strategy needs both: Facebook for the social graph, LinkedIn for the profession.
FINDING 03
Algorithmic recommendation is the gate to cross-border reach.
Claim
“Suggested” traffic drives U.S. share.
Evidence
Dose-responsive: 50%→47%, 60%→70%, 78%→82% U.S. audience as Suggested share rose.
Interpretation
Designing deliberately for recommendation is a repeatable route to reach, without ad spend.
FINDING 04
The campaign converged on its thesis by the close.
Claim
The argument’s arc showed in the audience data.
Evidence
The closing post was the only untagged article post where U.S. exceeded Bangladesh, 48.5% > 39.3%.
Interpretation
Sustained binational framing built a genuinely two-country audience. The data followed the argument.
Secondary observationsPatterns that shaped practice, below the level of a headline finding, but replicable.
Outlet credibility outweighs editorial effort
Daily Star pieces out-reached Eastern Echo even where the U.S. writing was arguably stronger, 12,775 vs ~1,260 median Facebook views. Placement in a trusted outlet amplifies in ways craft on a single piece cannot.
A branded template can stand in for an outlet
The no-outlet earthquake Special Report sat in the same LinkedIn range as published chapters (1,013 vs 1,240-1,565 impressions), where content routes by topic, design under a credible brand can substitute for institutional authority.
Platform-native optimization beats cross-posting
Music on Instagram, PDF uploads on LinkedIn, collaborator and location tags on Facebook, small native adjustments with outsized organic-reach effects. Content that looks imported performs worse.
Four more mechanismsAdditional replicable levers documented in the campaign appendices, each activated organically, with no ad spend or targeting.
The Reel-format flip
Same chapter, same week: switching Chapter 2 to a Reel flipped the audience from 63.8% Bangladesh to 75% U.S. Video format triggers topic-routing, the same lever as “Suggested”.
The place-name effect
Putting “Michigan” in the headline produced the closest U.S./Bangladesh gap of any untagged article (40.2% U.S.), a named geography is an explicit location signal to the algorithm.
Regional bleed by topic
Topic predicts tail-country audiences: India 7.6% on the earthquake piece, Canada 20% on U.S. messaging, Saudi Arabia on Bangladesh literacy, each surfaced without targeting.
Cold-start growth from zero
Built with no pre-existing audience: ↑2,121% Facebook views and ↑3,536% LinkedIn impressions across the eight-week window.
